Privacy policy for EarLab Last updated: 25 October 2026 Controller and contact Alex Ovcinnikov Contact: https://github.com/krixoko/earlab-privacy/issues (please do not post personal data in public requests) What this is EarLab checks Bluetooth headphones and speakers: sound checks, tone generation, channel balance, frequency response, delay, noise cancelling, audio-video sync and a microphone recording. The evaluation takes place on your device. EarLab has no user account, no servers of its own and no analytics or tracking services. The app uses an internet connection only for advertising (see "Advertising"). Microphone and audio For measurements and the microphone recording, EarLab asks for microphone access. Recordings last at most 30 seconds and are never played back automatically. The audio stays in memory until you replace it or the app ends. A file is only saved when you export a WAV file yourself. A saved report contains level values and measurement results, not the recording. EarLab does not record phone calls. Microphone audio is never sent to Google or anyone else. Bluetooth and devices EarLab reads which audio devices are connected and shows their names and properties. On Android 12 and later, Android requires the "Nearby devices" permission for this. The app does not change the volume. Reports, profiles and settings Saved reports, headphone profiles and settings are kept in the app's private storage (database and preferences). A report contains measurement values, the time, the name of the device used, and the manufacturer, model, Android version and app version of your phone. You can delete reports in the app. Automatic Android backup is turned off for EarLab. "Clear storage" in Android or uninstalling removes all local data. Exports You can export reports as PDF, CSV or JSON and recordings as WAV. You choose the destination yourself in the Android document picker or share sheet. A cloud service or app chosen there may process the file under its own rules; EarLab does not upload it itself. Files prepared for sharing are kept in the private cache and removed after about a day. Complaint report and photos For the optional complaint report you can pick up to three photos with the Android photo picker. EarLab reads them once to create the PDF and does not keep them. Optional features Connection monitoring: when you start it, a visible foreground service with a notification runs and logs the time and device name of connection drops. Background dropout alerts are off by default. If you turn them on, EarLab stores the time and device name of Bluetooth connects and disconnects and shows notifications (Android asks for the notification permission). You can stop both at any time. The widget and quick tile only start a local quick check. Advertising (Google AdMob) EarLab shows ads from Google's AdMob service: a banner on the overview screens (Tests, Connection, Reports, Profiles, Trends) and occasionally a full-screen ad after a saved report, at most after every third report and at most every five minutes. No ads appear during a measurement or recording, in the settings or in the complaint report. To load ads, the app connects to the internet; for this it holds the permissions INTERNET, ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE and AD_ID. Depending on your choice, Google may process data such as the advertising ID, IP address, device and usage information to deliver ads, prevent fraud and measure their effect. Google is responsible for this under https://policies.google.com/privacy. Your measurements, reports, device names and recordings are never sent to Google. Locally, the app only stores a counter and the time of the last full-screen ad to limit how often it appears. Consent: at startup and where required by law (EEA, United Kingdom), the app asks for your choice through Google's consent platform (UMP). Without consent, no ads are requested. You can change your choice in the settings under "Ad privacy settings". You can reset or delete the advertising ID in the Android settings at any time. No transfer to the developer Apart from the advertising data processed by Google as described above, the app does not transmit any personal data to the developer or other companies. Contact through GitHub If you contact the developer through GitHub, GitHub processes your details under its own privacy policy; requests there are publicly visible. The developer uses them only to handle your request (Art. 6(1)(b) or (f) GDPR). Your rights Where the developer processes personal data about you, you may request access, rectification, erasure, restriction of processing or data portability, and object to processing based on legitimate interests. You may also complain to a data protection authority. Google Play and Android Google Play, Android and possibly your device manufacturer process data under their own privacy policies. This policy describes processing by EarLab.
